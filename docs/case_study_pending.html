Turning Demand Forecasts into Inventory Decisions

A constrained inventory decision experiment with explicit operating assumptions.

Empirical study pending. The implementation is tested with artificial software fixtures. Official M5 data requires authenticated access. This page is an implementation brief, not evidence of measured retail impact.

Business problem

A shared purchasing budget creates trade-offs across products. Better forecasts are useful only if they lead to better decisions under uncertainty. The study compares integer scenario optimization with historical-average and forecast-based order-up-to policies.

Data

The designated source is official Walmart M5 unit sales, calendar and selling prices. Sales may be censored by inventory availability. Unit purchasing costs, initial stocks, holding costs and shortage penalties are explicitly modeled because the dataset does not establish them.

Forecasting and optimization

Chronological windows separate residual calibration, model selection and held-out evaluation. Three simple forecasting baselines compete with direct global gradient boosting. Integer orders minimize assumed procurement plus expected holding and unmet-sales-proxy penalties under a shared budget.

Decision evaluation

Every policy receives the same initial stock, cash constraint, arrival delay and held-out sales proxy. One-factor sensitivities vary budget, penalties, holding costs, lead time, uncertainty and initial inventory. The optimizer is verified against exhaustive enumeration on a small artificial problem.

Limitations and business implications

The first analytical release uses only three calibration blocks and two final test windows; it cannot establish population-level significance or calibrated tail-risk protection. Zero terminal salvage and assumed shortage penalties can materially affect which policy looks best. No recommendation should be presented as deployment-ready until the real-data and sensitivity results are inspected.
