Turning Demand Forecasts into Inventory Decisions

Better forecasts are useful only when they support better decisions. This first real-data study found an important gap between the two.

The business question

How should a retailer allocate a limited purchasing budget across products when future sales are uncertain? RetailOS connects chronological forecasting, integer inventory optimization, held-out policy replay and grounded review. Its objective is to test decision value, rather than assume that a more complex model creates it.

Data and scope

The designated source is official Walmart M5 unit sales, calendar and selling prices. The study selected two eligible products per store–department across TX_1, CA_1, WI_1: 42 series and 81,522 selected historical rows. Eligibility requires a listed-price history of at least 84 days at the first forecast origin; zero-sales shares and volume use only training information. This is a deliberate contrast of demand patterns, not a probability sample of Walmart.

The two held-out episodes cover 2016-03-28 through 2016-05-22, with initial inventory reset for each episode. Unit sales are a potentially censored demand proxy. Purchase cost, initial stock, holding cost, shortage penalty, budget and lead time are assumptions.

Forecasting

Three earlier 28-day origins provide residual blocks; a fourth chooses the forecast model; the final two evaluate outcomes. Gradient boosting uses origin-available lag summaries, last observed price and known-calendar assumptions. Training targets end by each origin. It is compared with active-history mean, seasonal naive and trailing mean forecasts.

Inventory optimization

A shared budget constrains nonnegative integer orders. One order arrives after an assumed two-day lead time during each 28-day episode. The objective is procurement plus expected daily holding and unmet-sales-proxy penalty across generated scenarios. Default purchase cost is 65% of last observed selling price; holding is 0.2% of assumed unit cost per day; shortage penalty is three times unit cost; budget is 75% of historical purchasing need; initial inventory is three days of trailing sales. There is no terminal salvage credit.

Both simple policies receive identical cash, stock, arrival and accounting assumptions. Infeasible targets use the same proportional reduction and integer rounding rule. Neither policy generation nor the optimizer sees future observed sales.

Across two paired test episodes, optimized cost was $24,731.97 versus $22,088.62 for trailing-average orders and $22,161.68 for forecast order-up-to. The increase is 11.97% against the first baseline and 11.60% against the second, using each baseline's pooled cost as denominator.

An independent physical replay matched all 6 saved policy-window costs. Against trailing-average orders, purchasing expenditure changed by $-0.039, holding by +$18.86, and unmet-proxy penalties by +$2,624.52. More inventory remained while more sales-proxy units went unmet: an allocation mismatch under this evaluation.

Diagnosis and sensitivity

The original scenario-expected cost replay favored the optimizer, while held-out costs favored the simple policies. Only three unique residual blocks support eight sampled paths. The empirical 10–90% daily scenario envelope covered 38.52% of held-out SKU-days; it is not a calibrated 80% prediction interval. Inadequate scenario representation is a plausible contributor, not an established sole cause.

Optimization was more expensive than trailing-average orders in 12 of 13 alternative one-factor scenarios. The favorable shortage-penalty multiple of 1 changes the business objective and is not a reason to tune assumptions to manufacture success. The scenario variants are correlated, not independent replications.

Grounded decision support

The application uses deterministic explanations of verified spend, constraints and risk flags. It does not use an LLM to select orders, and it does not invent confidence scores. A successful mathematical solve remains separate from an evidence-supported business recommendation.

Limits and next experiments

  • Two held-out blocks and three calibration blocks do not establish population significance or robust tail protection.
  • Observed sales can understate demand; actual inventory and operating costs are unavailable.
  • Zero terminal salvage, central budget pooling and episode resets materially shape interpretation.
  • Compare point-forecast optimization, improved uncertainty construction and simple policies on pre-test development windows. Select the decision rule before evaluating new holdout periods.
  • Already inspected test windows may support exploratory reanalysis; they are no longer an untouched confirmation set.

Actual application

The current dashboard renders the inspected first-run results and keeps REVIEW REQUIRED visible. This is an actual running Streamlit capture, not a mockup.

Business implication

The verified accomplishment is an end-to-end decision experiment that can reject an attractive but unsupported optimization recommendation. Better point accuracy was insufficient for better inventory decisions. The present evidence supports improving scenario validation and retaining a transparent baseline, not deploying the optimizer or claiming Walmart savings.
